1. METHODOLOGY OVERVIEW
-------------------------

SMSS uses the Scrum framework, an Agile methodology that delivers work in
short, time-boxed cycles called Sprints. Instead of building the entire
application before showing anything to users, Scrum releases working features
every two weeks so feedback can be gathered early and the product can be
adjusted based on real use.

Why Scrum suits SMSS:

  Iterative delivery  : Ship working features every 2 weeks rather than a
                        single big release after months of work.

  Early feedback      : The business owner tests features as they are built
                        and catches misunderstandings before they compound.

  Flexibility         : Requirements captured in handwritten notes are still
                        evolving. Scrum expects and accommodates change.

  Transparency        : Sprint boards make progress visible to the owner and
                        stakeholders at all times.

  Risk reduction      : Small increments mean bugs surface early rather than
                        piling up at the end of the project.


2. SCRUM TEAM ROLES
---------------------

Product Owner
  Who     : The business owner / project sponsor
  Does    : Owns and prioritises the Product Backlog. Defines what needs to
            be built and why. Accepts or rejects completed features. Represents
            the end-user's needs in every sprint discussion.

Scrum Master
  Who     : Lead Developer
  Does    : Facilitates all Scrum ceremonies. Removes blockers that slow the
            team down. Ensures the team follows the Scrum process. Protects
            the team from unplanned scope being added mid-sprint.

Development Team
  Who     : Developer(s)
  Does    : Designs, codes, tests, and delivers features. The team is
            self-organising and cross-functional.

Note: For a solo or two-person team, one person may hold multiple roles.
The Scrum Master duties still need to happen — assign them clearly.


3. SCRUM CEREMONIES
---------------------

Sprint Planning
  When     : Day 1 of every sprint (Monday morning)
  Duration : 1 to 2 hours
  Purpose  : Review the top backlog items. Select the stories that fit within
             the sprint capacity. Break each story into tasks and estimate
             effort. Confirm the sprint goal as one clear sentence.

Daily Standup
  When     : Every morning throughout the sprint
  Duration : 15 minutes maximum
  Purpose  : Each team member answers three questions:
               1. What did I complete since the last standup?
               2. What will I work on today?
               3. Do I have any blockers?

Sprint Review
  When     : Last day of every sprint (Friday afternoon)
  Duration : 1 hour
  Purpose  : Demo completed features to the Product Owner. Collect feedback.
             Accept or reject stories. Review what was and was not completed.

Sprint Retrospective
  When     : Immediately after the Sprint Review
  Duration : 45 minutes
  Purpose  : Reflect on how the team worked together, not what was built.
               What went well? What should we improve? What should we start
               doing differently? Record 2 to 3 action items for next sprint.

Backlog Refinement
  When     : Mid-sprint (Wednesday of the first week)
  Duration : 45 minutes
  Purpose  : Clarify upcoming backlog stories. Estimate or re-estimate story
             points. Ensure the next sprint's stories are ready to be picked up.


4. SPRINT CONFIGURATION
-------------------------

Sprint length            : 2 weeks
Estimation unit          : Story Points using Fibonacci scale
                           (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13)
Target velocity          : 20 to 30 story points per sprint
                           (adjust after Sprint 1 based on actual output)
Sprint start day         : Monday
Sprint end day           : Friday of week 2
Working days             : 5 days per week


5. STORY POINT SCALE
----------------------

1 point   : Trivial. Under 1 hour. E.g. update a text label, fix a typo.
2 points  : Simple. 1 to 3 hours. E.g. add a form field, update a DB query.
3 points  : Small. About half a day. E.g. build a simple CRUD screen.
5 points  : Medium. 1 to 2 days. E.g. full feature with API, UI, and validation.
8 points  : Large. 2 to 4 days. E.g. complex feature with auth, realtime, tests.
13 points : Too large. Must be broken into smaller stories before the sprint.


6. SPRINT BOARD COLUMNS
-------------------------

  BACKLOG       --> Future work not yet committed to any sprint.
  TO DO         --> Stories committed to the current sprint, not yet started.
  IN PROGRESS   --> Actively being coded right now.
  IN REVIEW     --> Pull request open or feature in QA testing.
  DONE          --> Meets the Definition of Done. Accepted by Product Owner.


7. USER STORY FORMAT
----------------------

All backlog items are written as user stories using this format:

  As a [role],
  I want to [action or feature],
  So that [benefit or business value].

  Acceptance Criteria:
    - Given [context], when [action], then [expected result].
    - Given [context], when [action], then [expected result].

  Story Points : [1 / 2 / 3 / 5 / 8]
  Priority     : Must Have / Should Have / Could Have / Won't Have


Example:

  As an owner, I want to register a new centre with a name, location,
  and centre number, so that I can begin assigning workers and tracking
  that centre's operations.

  Acceptance Criteria:
    - Given I am logged in as owner, when I tap Add Centre, then I see
      a form with the required fields.
    - Given I fill in all required fields, when I submit, then the new
      centre appears in my list.
    - Given I enter a duplicate centre number, when I submit, then I see
      an error message saying the number is already taken.

  Story Points : 5
  Priority     : Must Have


8. DEFINITION OF READY
------------------------

A story is ready to enter a sprint only when:

  - It has a clear user story statement (As a / I want / So that)
  - Acceptance criteria are fully defined
  - It has been estimated in story points
  - Dependencies are identified
  - A UI wireframe or screen sketch exists if UI changes are involved
  - It can realistically be completed within one sprint


9. RELEASE STRATEGY
---------------------

Sprint 0        : Setup sprint. No releasable feature. Infrastructure only.
Sprints 1 to 5  : Development sprints. Internal test builds via EAS Preview.
Sprint 6        : Hardening sprint. First production build submitted to
                  Google Play internal testing track.
Post-MVP        : Ongoing sprints for enhancements and new features.

Release cadence:
At the end of every two sprints, a release candidate is built from the main
branch and distributed via Expo EAS internal testing to the Product Owner
for sign-off before any wider distribution.
